Day 1 — Two ways to model an outbreak

Wednesday 25 November 2026 · 09:00–12:00 and 13:00–15:00

Learning outcomes

  • Write down an SIR model and explain every term.
  • Compute \(R_0\) and the herd immunity threshold, and find equilibria.
  • Explain why an outbreak with \(R_0 > 1\) can still die out.
  • Compute the probability that a resistance mutation rescues a population under treatment.
  • See that the last two are the same calculation.

09:00 · How this course works

Projects in teams of 3–5. Three pitches before the final talk. Grading, AI policy.

09:15 · Lecture 1 — Compartment models

  • What epidemiological models are for: understanding vs. prediction; “all models are wrong”.
  • SIR as ordinary differential equations: mass action, rates, mean infectious period \(1/\gamma\).
  • Solving ODEs numerically; equilibria; the phase plane (\(S\)–\(I\)).
  • \(R_0 = \beta/\gamma\). Infections rise as long as \(S > 1/R_0\), which gives the herd immunity threshold \(1 - 1/R_0\).
  • SIR with births and deaths: endemic equilibrium, damped oscillations.
  • Brief tour of extensions: SEIR, vector-borne, cholera (environmental reservoir).

10:30 · Lecture 2 — When chance decides

The first infected person in a new population and the first resistant bacterium in a treated patient are in the same situation. Both are a single lineage that grows on average but can easily be lost by chance.

  • Branching processes: each individual leaves a random number of “offspring” (infections, daughter cells).
  • Haldane (1927): the extinction probability \(\eta\) solves \(\eta = G(\eta)\), where \(G\) is the offspring generating function.
  • Epidemic takeoff: in SIR, \(P(\text{takeoff}) = 1 - 1/R_0\).
  • Evolution of resistance (evolutionary rescue): a population under treatment declines at rate \(r\), and resistant mutants arise at rate \(u\) and grow at rate \(s\).
    • Establishment probability of one mutant \(\approx 2s\).
    • Rescue probability \(\approx 1 - \exp(-2Nus/r)\) (Orr & Unckless 2014).
  • The unifying rule: \(P(\text{establish}) \approx 2 \times (\text{mean} - 1) / \text{variance}\) of the offspring number.
